# Standing vs Max Vertical Jump — Key Distinctions Explained

> **Source:** https://www.tiktok.com/@jumpsbychi/video/7564848380271283487
> **Date:** 2026-06-01

CONFIDENCE: medium

## Core Principles

- "Vertical jump" is not a single metric — it simply means jumping for height (jumping vertically)
- Different types of vertical jumps measure similar but distinct athletic abilities
- Neither type is more "legitimate" than the other; they are different metrics, not a hierarchy

## Key Frameworks & Progressions

**Types of Vertical Jumps:**
- **Standing Vertical Jump** — measured from a stationary position; used by the NFL Combine
- **Max Vertical / Approach Vertical Jump** — measured with a running approach to maximise height; measured by the NBA alongside standing vertical
- **Hands-on-Hips Jump** — variation removing arm swing contribution
- **Seated Vertical Jump** — removes lower-body pre-loading contribution

**Key Institutional Standards:**
- NFL Combine → standing vertical only
- NBA Draft Combine → measures *both* standing and max vertical

## Things to Avoid

- Assuming a running/approach jump is "cheating" or not a real vertical jump
- Treating "vertical jump" as a single universal metric without specifying the type
- Dismissing one jump type as inferior to another — they measure related but distinct qualities

## Key Takeaways

- Always clarify *which type* of vertical jump is being referenced — context determines meaning
- Max vertical and standing vertical are complementary metrics, not competing ones
- The distinction matters practically: different sports organisations prioritise different measurements
- Conflating jump types leads to misleading comparisons between athletes
